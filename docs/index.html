TLD-Net: Transparency-Luminance Disentangled Network for Unified Industrial Gas Leak and Fire Monitoring

Muhammad Ayaz1, Sareer Ul Amin1, Muhammad Munsif2, Salman Khan3, Jung Hun Ryu1, Sanghyun Seo1,*
1 Chung-Ang University, Republic of Korea  |  2 Ulsan National Institute of Science and Technology (UNIST), Republic of Korea  |  3 Oxford Brookes University, United Kingdom
* Corresponding Author  ·  sanghyun@cau.ac.kr
Paper (Soon) Code (Soon) UIHB Dataset (Soon)

Abstract

Gas leaks and fire outbreaks are critical industrial hazards that frequently co-occur, posing serious risks to infrastructure and safety. Existing perception systems address these hazards using separate task-specific pipelines with incompatible sensing modalities, resulting in redundant sensors, duplicated computation, and fragmented scene understanding. These systems are further limited by the transparency-luminance ambiguity, where semi-transparent gas plumes exhibit weak, diffuse boundaries in infrared imagery, while fire regions produce extreme luminance variation that degrades feature representation. To address these challenges, we propose the Transparency-Luminance Disentangled Network (TLD-Net), a unified instance segmentation framework for simultaneous gas leak and fire monitoring from multimodal industrial imagery. TLD-Net comprises three complementary modules: Scale-Recurrent Pyramid Enhancement (SRPE), which iteratively refines multi-scale features through ConvGRU-based recurrent context propagation to improve small-object representation, Transparency-Aware Gas Detection (TAGD), which estimates mask-guided displacement fields to compensate for refractive distortion and recover transparent gas boundaries, providing a measurable indicator of local distortion severity, and an Asymmetric Causal Reasoning Module (ACRM), which models the directional physical dependency between gas and fire through gradient-isolated causal gating. To support this framework, we introduce the Unified Industrial Hazard Benchmark (UIHB), comprising 7,977 images with 11,397 instance-level annotations. On this benchmark, TLD-Net achieves 47.0% mask mAP, outperforming the baseline by 2.6 percentage points overall and 5.2 percentage points at AP75, with TensorRT INT8 deployment reaching 23.5 FPS on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin, demonstrating practicality for real-time industrial hazard monitoring.

Highlights

Scale-Recurrent Pyramid Enhancement

A recurrent ConvGRU-based multi-scale neck replacing static FPN, letting fine pyramid levels progressively accumulate semantic context over three passes for better small and partially occluded hazard detection.

Transparency-Aware Gas Detection

Learns a dense displacement field to model refractive distortion from semi-transparent gas plumes, producing distortion-aligned features for tighter, more physically grounded gas boundary masks.

Asymmetric Causal Reasoning Module

Gradient-isolated causal gating encodes the physical gas→fire precedence, letting gas context selectively enhance fire features without entangling the two hazard representations.

47.0% mAP · 23.5 FPS

+2.6 pts over the strongest baseline overall and +5.2 pts at AP75, with real-time TensorRT INT8 edge deployment on an NVIDIA Jetson AGX Orin.

UIHB Benchmark

7,977 images and 11,397 instance-level annotations across co-registered infrared and RGB sources, with Jaccard > 0.85 inter-annotator agreement — the first cross-hazard instance segmentation benchmark.

Zero-Shot Generalization

43.8% mAP on unseen gas-only data and 41.6% mAP on unseen fire-only data, confirming domain-robust representations rather than overfitting to UIHB's co-occurrence statistics.

Architecture

Overview of TLD-Net. A ResNet backbone feeds the SRPE neck, producing a recurrently refined five-level pyramid. TAGD estimates gas distortion from P2 and injects distortion features into the mask branch. ACRM causally gates fire features using a gas objectness prior. A three-stage Cascade R-CNN head produces per-instance masks for gas and fire simultaneously.

Evolution of the P2 feature map through SRPE's recurrent refinement across three representative scenes. From left: input image, FPN initialization, and successive ConvGRU passes (T=1, 2, 3). Fine-grained, blocky activations present at initialization are progressively smoothed and consolidated into coherent regional structure by the final pass, reflecting the multi-hop propagation of semantic context from coarser pyramid levels.
